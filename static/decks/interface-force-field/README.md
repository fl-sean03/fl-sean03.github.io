# Interface Force Field

This presentation is a beginner’s guide to simulating where molecules meet solid materials. It shows how a force field describes atoms with balls, springs and charges, why the Interface Force Field, or IFF, was needed, how it tunes a solid such as rhodium to its measured surface energy, and three published studies in which IFF was compared with experiments.

Open `index.html` for the viewer. It carries its fonts and pictures inside it and loads only the slide 2 animation from `assets/`. Press T, or add `#read` to the address, for the reading version with the speaker’s notes. It opens by itself on a phone held upright. The editable PowerPoint and the PDF with selectable text are in `downloads/`, and `presenter-notes.md` holds the notes with their sources.

Every picture was drawn for this presentation from open crystal structures, published parameters and molecules built with RDKit. Pictures of molecules on surfaces are illustrations, not simulation snapshots, and the animation on slide 2 illustrates an idea under a simple rule and is not a simulation from the paper. Published results are quoted from the cited papers and were not rerun.

`source/README.md` explains how every picture was made, how to rebuild and check the presentation, and how it was revised.
